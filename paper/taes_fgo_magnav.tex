{Factor-Graph Aeromagnetic Compensation for Magnetic-Anomaly Navigation}

\begin{abstract}
Airborne magnetic-anomaly navigation bounds inertial drift in GNSS-denied flight
by matching a scalar magnetometer against a magnetic-anomaly map. Its central
obstacle is aeromagnetic interference: the aircraft's own field must be
compensated online when no calibration flight is available (the cold-start
regime). A recent approach augments an extended Kalman filter (EKF) with online
Tolles--Lawson (TL) coefficients and a residual neural network. We show that a
factor-graph formulation reaches the same accuracy without a neural network by
carrying the TL coefficients as graph variables of a fixed-lag sliding-window
smoother, so the compensation adapts along the flight. On the primary evaluation
line of the SGL~2020 dataset the sliding-window smoother matches or beats a
reproduced online EKF+TL+NN and the values published for it. Across five long
survey lines from three flights and two maps, it improves on a causal online-TL
EKF on \num{8} of \num{9} cold-start cases and, importantly, remains bounded where
the causal filter diverges. We also give an exact linear condition for the joint
observability of compensation and navigation, from which an exogeneity design
rule for the compensation features follows.
\end{abstract}

Magnetic-anomaly navigation, aeromagnetic compensation, Tolles--Lawson,

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{M}{agnetic}-anomaly navigation estimates aircraft position by
matching a measured scalar magnetic field against a surveyed crustal-anomaly map,
providing an absolute position reference that bounds inertial drift when GNSS is
denied~\cite{canciani2016,canciani2017}. The public benchmark for the problem is
the Signal Enhancement for Magnetic Navigation (SGL~2020) dataset~\cite{gnadt2020}.

The dominant error source is the aircraft, not the map: permanent and induced
magnetization and eddy-current fields perturb the scalar measurement by hundreds
of nanotesla, dwarfing the anomaly signal. Classical aeromagnetic compensation
uses the Tolles--Lawson (TL) model~\cite{tolles1950}, a linear regression of the
interference onto attitude-dependent terms built from a three-axis fluxgate, with
coefficients fit on a dedicated calibration flight. When no calibration flight is
available---the operationally important cold-start regime---the coefficients must
be learned online, jointly with navigation. A state-of-the-art approach augments
an EKF with online TL coefficients and a residual neural network (NN), the latter
learned online and stabilized by a dedicated training design~\cite{hager2026}.

We ask whether the neural network is necessary, and show it is not. We treat the
TL coefficients as time-varying variables of a factor graph and solve the joint
estimation as a fixed-lag sliding-window smoother; the per-window relinearization
supplies the time-varying compensation that the competing method obtains from an
online network.

We are explicit about scope. For the linear--Gaussian INS error model the batch
maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) estimate on the factor-graph chain equals the
Rauch--Tung--Striebel (RTS) smoother~\cite{rauch1965}; smoothing alone is not our
subject. The contributions are: an online-TL compensation cast as a fixed-lag
factor-graph smoother, an NN-free alternative to online EKF+TL+NN
(Section~\ref{sec:method}); a reproduced---not merely cited---EKF+TL+NN baseline
and a five-line breadth study on real data (Section~\ref{sec:results}); and an
exact observability condition for joint compensation and navigation with an
exogeneity corollary (Section~\ref{sec:observability}).

\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{INS error state and measurement}
We use the Pinson INS error model augmented with a first-order Gauss--Markov
(FOGM) catch-all magnetic state, error state $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$, and
linearized dynamics $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t\mathbf{x}_t+\mathbf{w}_t$,
$\mathbf{w}_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{Q}_t)$. The scalar magnetometer measurement
is
\begin{equation}
z_t = h(\mathbf{p}_t) + c_t + \eta_t, \qquad \eta_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,R),
\label{eq:meas}
\end{equation}
where $h$ interpolates the anomaly map (with IGRF core) at position $\mathbf{p}_t$
and $c_t$ is the aircraft interference. A position error $\delta\mathbf{p}$
perturbs the prediction by $\nabla h\cdot\delta\mathbf{p}$; this gradient is the
sole source of horizontal position information.

\subsection{Tolles--Lawson compensation}
The interference is modeled as $c_t=\mathbf{A}_t^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}_t$,
where $\mathbf{A}_t$ is the TL basis row (permanent, induced, and eddy-current
terms, and an optional bias) formed from the fluxgate at time $t$~\cite{tolles1950}
and $\boldsymbol{\beta}_t$ are the coefficients. Cold-start operation makes
$\boldsymbol{\beta}$ a slowly time-varying state.

\subsection{Batch MAP estimate}
Over a window of $N$ epochs the MAP estimate minimizes
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
J=\;&\lVert\mathbf{x}_1-\mathbf{x}_0\rVert^2_{\mathbf{P}_0^{-1}}
+\sum_{t=1}^{N-1}\lVert\mathbf{x}_{t+1}-\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t\mathbf{x}_t\rVert^2_{\mathbf{Q}_t^{-1}}\\
&+\sum_{t=1}^{N}\rho\!\left(\frac{z_t-h_t(\mathbf{x}_t)}{\sqrt{R}}\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
with $\rho$ a possibly robust kernel. For $\rho(u)=u^2$ and the linear--Gaussian
chain the minimizer of~\eqref{eq:cost} equals the RTS smoother~\cite{rauch1965};
we solve it by an iterated RTS pass or, equivalently, by a sparse
Gauss--Newton/QR (square-root SAM) step~\cite{dellaert2017}, with iteratively
reweighted least squares for robust $\rho$.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Online TL coefficients as graph variables}
We append $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ to the error state, so the measurement factor
becomes $z_t=\mathbf{A}_t^{\!\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}_t+h(\mathbf{p}_t)+S_t+\eta_t$
with $S_t$ the FOGM state, and the measurement Jacobian row is
$\mathbf{H}_t=[\,\mathbf{g}_t^{\!\top}\;\;\mathbf{0}\;\;\mathbf{A}_t^{\!\top}\;\;1\,]$,
$\mathbf{g}_t=\partial h/\partial\mathbf{p}$. Because the batch solution uses the
entire window, early navigation states benefit from calibration information that
only becomes observable after later maneuvers---a property a causal filter lacks.

\subsection{Fixed-lag sliding window}
A single batch over a long line forces one static $\boldsymbol{\beta}$, which
underfits time-varying interference. We instead process the flight in overlapping
windows of length $L_w$ with overlap $L_o$, each a batch factor graph, in the
manner of incremental smoothing~\cite{kaess2012}. A window commits only its
leading stride $L_w-L_o$ epochs and carries the full smoothed state and covariance
forward as the prior for the next window; the trailing overlap serves as smoother
look-ahead. The compensation thus adapts along the flight while navigation stays
continuous across windows.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.98\columnwidth]{figs/fig_window.pdf}
\caption{Each window commits its leading stride and passes the full smoothed
state and covariance forward as the prior for the next window.}
\label{fig:window}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Physical sensor-error factors}
Beyond TL we cast physically-motivated scalar-magnetometer error terms as graph
variables: an optically-pumped-magnetometer heading error as a Fourier series in
the sensor--field angle $\theta$, a fluxgate hard-iron bias, a linear drift, and a
dead-zone heteroscedastic measurement weight. A position-only grid or point-mass
estimator cannot carry such sensor-error states; the graph formulation can.

\section{Observability of Joint Compensation and Navigation}
\label{sec:observability}
Whether position can be recovered while the compensation is estimated jointly is a
property of the measurement geometry. Over a window let $\mathbf{G}$ have rows
$\mathbf{g}_t^{\!\top}$ (the map-gradient/position sensitivity) and let
$\boldsymbol{\Psi}$ have rows $\mathbf{A}_t^{\!\top}$ (the TL regressor). To
isolate the position--compensation coupling, restrict~\eqref{eq:meas} to the
$(\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\boldsymbol{\beta})$ subspace, giving the window model
$\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{G}\,\delta\mathbf{p}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}\,\delta\boldsymbol{\beta}+\boldsymbol{\eta}$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:obs}
A position perturbation $\delta\mathbf{p}$ is indistinguishable from a
compensation adjustment---hence unobservable from map matching over the
window---if and only if $\mathbf{G}\,\delta\mathbf{p}\in\mathrm{range}(\boldsymbol{\Psi})$.
Consequently the joint state $(\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\boldsymbol{\beta})$ is
observable if and only if
$\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{G})\cap\mathrm{range}(\boldsymbol{\Psi})=\{\mathbf{0}\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Two joint states $(\delta\mathbf{p},\delta\boldsymbol{\beta})$ and
$(\delta\mathbf{p}',\delta\boldsymbol{\beta}')$ produce identical noise-free
predictions iff
$\mathbf{G}(\delta\mathbf{p}-\delta\mathbf{p}')=\boldsymbol{\Psi}(\delta\boldsymbol{\beta}'-\delta\boldsymbol{\beta})$,
i.e.\ iff $\mathbf{G}(\delta\mathbf{p}-\delta\mathbf{p}')\in\mathrm{range}(\boldsymbol{\Psi})$.
A nonzero position difference with this property exists iff
$\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{G})\cap\mathrm{range}(\boldsymbol{\Psi})\neq\{\mathbf{0}\}$,
which is exactly the negation of the stated condition; equivalently the stacked
Jacobian $[\,\mathbf{G}\;\;\boldsymbol{\Psi}\,]$ is column-rank-deficient.
\end{IEEEproof}

\begin{corollary}[Exogeneity]\label{cor:exo}
If $\mathbf{A}_t$ is a function of aircraft attitude/fluxgate alone---exogenous to
position---then $\mathrm{range}(\boldsymbol{\Psi})$ is generically transverse to
the map-gradient subspace $\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{G})$ and the joint problem is
observable at any basis dimension. If instead a compensation feature contains the
map value $h(\mathbf{p})$ (for example the scalar magnetometer itself), its column
aligns with $\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{G})$, forcing the intersection nonzero and
collapsing observability.
\end{corollary}

Proposition~\ref{prop:obs} is exact but non-constructive: it does not yield a
reliable scalar screening statistic, and in our experiments correlation-based
proxies for the intersection did not generalize across estimators. The actionable
consequence is Corollary~\ref{cor:exo}: drive the compensation from exogenous
features, which every estimator in Section~\ref{sec:results} does.

\section{Reproduced Online EKF+TL+NN Baseline}\label{sec:baseline}
To compare against~\cite{hager2026} on equal footing we re-run, rather than cite,
an online EKF with TL basis features and NN weights carried as EKF states and
learned online. Reproducing the cold start required three fixes, each addressing a
distinct failure: initializing the NN output bias to the onboard-estimated
interference offset; restricting the NN inputs to the attitude-derived TL basis
and excluding the scalar magnetometer (consistent with Corollary~\ref{cor:exo});
and de-trusting the map through the cold-start transient. The resulting filter
lands inside the paper's published band and serves as our baseline.

\section{Experiments and Results}\label{sec:results}
All experiments use the public SGL~2020 dataset~\cite{gnadt2020} and the Ottawa
anomaly maps, and are produced by continuous-integration scripts on the code
branch. The metric is horizontal distance-RMS (DRMS) after a \SI{10}{\minute}
warm-up. Cold start means TL coefficients initialized to zero with no calibration
flight; the EKF and FGO comparisons use identical noise settings so that only the
estimator structure differs.

\subsection{Batch estimator on real data}
On line 1003.02 (Flt1003, \SI{63}{\minute}) with the compensated stinger
magnetometer, the batch factor graph improves horizontal DRMS from the EKF's
\SI{28.3}{\meter} to \SI{14.0}{\meter} (Huber kernel), against
\SI{114.6}{\meter} free-inertial. On a \SI{10}{\minute} segment the iterated-RTS
and sparse-GN/QR solvers agree to within \SI{1}{\meter}, confirming both reach the
same MAP estimate.

\subsection{Sensor-error factors (simulation)}
On a simulated flight with injected heading error, hard-iron bias, drift, and
dead-zone noise (INS reference \SI{31.1}{\meter}), cumulatively adding the
sensor-error factors reduces DRMS from \SI{36.6}{\meter} (no sensor states, worse
than INS because unmodeled error corrupts aiding) to \SI{4.8}{\meter}. The drift
and one bias component are recovered cleanly, while the heading harmonics are
observability-limited because level flight sweeps the sensor--field angle by only
about \SI{12}{\degree}---an observability property, not an estimator defect.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
The advantage is not smoothing per se, which equals RTS in the linear--Gaussian
case, but the joint windowed estimation of a time-varying compensation with
navigation. A causal filter must commit online with no future context; under a
large uncompensated cold-start residual it attributes the mismatch to position and
diverges, as Table~\ref{tab:breadth} shows. The fixed-lag window defers
commitment. Proposition~\ref{prop:obs} explains when the joint problem is
well-posed and Corollary~\ref{cor:exo} gives the practical rule the results obey.
Limitations include single-run DRMS on five lines (Monte-Carlo statistics and the
full line set remain), the absence of a like-for-like grid/point-mass or
particle-filter baseline, and simulation-only sensor-error validation. Short lines
remain hard for every method.

\section{Conclusion}
Casting online Tolles--Lawson aeromagnetic compensation as time-varying
factor-graph variables in a fixed-lag sliding-window smoother yields a
neural-network-free cold-start calibrator that, on real SGL~2020 data, matches or
beats a reproduced online EKF+TL+NN on the primary line and generalizes across
five lines, three flights, and two maps, remaining bounded where the causal EKF
diverges. An exact observability condition and its exogeneity corollary explain
the behavior and guide the choice of compensation features.
