Factor-Graph Aeromagnetic Compensation for Airborne Magnetic-Anomaly Navigation
Anonymous
Manuscript draft. Reproducible code and experiments: MagNav.jl research branch claude/fgo-problem-research-bllgbr.
Abstract— Airborne magnetic-anomaly navigation bounds inertial drift in GNSS-denied flight by matching a scalar magnetometer against a magnetic-anomaly map. Its central obstacle is aeromagnetic interference: the aircraft’s own field must be compensated online when no calibration flight is available (the cold-start regime). A recent approach augments an extended Kalman filter (EKF) with online Tolles–Lawson (TL) coefficients and a residual neural network. We show that a factor-graph formulation reaches the same accuracy without a neural network by carrying the TL coefficients as graph variables of a fixed-lag sliding-window smoother, so the compensation adapts along the flight. On the primary evaluation line of the SGL 2020 dataset the sliding-window smoother matches or beats a reproduced online EKF+TL+NN and the values published for it. Across five long survey lines from three flights and two maps, it improves on a causal online-TL EKF on 8 of 9 cold-start cases and, importantly, remains bounded where the causal filter diverges. We also give an exact linear condition for the joint observability of compensation and navigation, from which an exogeneity design rule for the compensation features follows.
Index Terms— Magnetic-anomaly navigation, aeromagnetic compensation, Tolles–Lawson, factor-graph optimization, fixed-lag smoothing, GNSS-denied navigation.

I.  Introduction

MAGNETIC-anomaly navigation estimates aircraft position by matching a measured scalar magnetic field against a surveyed crustal-anomaly map, providing an absolute position reference that bounds inertial drift when GNSS is denied [1], [2]. The public benchmark for the problem is the Signal Enhancement for Magnetic Navigation (SGL 2020) dataset [3].

The dominant error source is the aircraft, not the map: permanent and induced magnetization and eddy-current fields perturb the scalar measurement by hundreds of nanotesla, dwarfing the anomaly signal. Classical aeromagnetic compensation uses the Tolles–Lawson (TL) model [4], a linear regression of the interference onto attitude-dependent terms built from a three-axis fluxgate, with coefficients fit on a dedicated calibration flight. When no calibration flight is available—the operationally important cold-start regime—the coefficients must be learned online, jointly with navigation. A state-of-the-art approach augments an EKF with online TL coefficients and a residual neural network (NN), the latter learned online and stabilized by a dedicated training design [5].

We ask whether the neural network is necessary, and show it is not. We treat the TL coefficients as time-varying variables of a factor graph and solve the joint estimation as a fixed-lag sliding-window smoother; the per-window relinearization supplies the time-varying compensation that the competing method obtains from an online network.

We are explicit about scope. For the linear–Gaussian INS error model the batch maximum-a-posteriori (MAP) estimate on the factor-graph chain equals the Rauch–Tung–Striebel (RTS) smoother [6]; smoothing alone is not our subject. The contributions are: an online-TL compensation cast as a fixed-lag factor-graph smoother, an NN-free alternative to online EKF+TL+NN (Section III); a reproduced—not merely cited—EKF+TL+NN baseline and a five-line breadth study on real data (Section VI); and an exact observability condition for joint compensation and navigation with an exogeneity corollary (Section IV).

II.  Problem Formulation

A. INS error state and measurement

We use the Pinson INS error model augmented with a first-order Gauss–Markov (FOGM) catch-all magnetic state, error state x ∈ ℝn, and linearized dynamics xt+1 = Φtxt + wt, wt ∼ 𝒩(0, Qt). The scalar magnetometer measurement is

(1) zt = h(pt) + ct + ηt,   ηt ∼ 𝒩(0, R),

where h interpolates the anomaly map (with IGRF core) at position pt and ct is the aircraft interference. A position error δp perturbs the prediction by ∇h·δp; this gradient is the sole source of horizontal position information.

B. Tolles–Lawson compensation

The interference is modeled as ct = At⊤βt, where At is the TL basis row (permanent, induced, and eddy-current terms, and an optional bias) formed from the fluxgate at time t [4] and βt are the coefficients. Cold-start operation makes β a slowly time-varying state.

C. Batch MAP estimate

Over a window of N epochs the MAP estimate minimizes

(2) J = ‖x1−x0‖2P0−1 + ∑t ‖xt+1−Φtxt‖2Qt−1 + ∑t ρ((zt−ht)/√R),

with ρ a possibly robust kernel. For ρ(u)=u2 and the linear–Gaussian chain the minimizer of (2) equals the RTS smoother [6]; we solve it by an iterated RTS pass or, equivalently, by a sparse Gauss–Newton/QR (square-root SAM) step [7], with iteratively reweighted least squares for robust ρ.

III.  Method

A. Online TL coefficients as graph variables

We append β to the error state, so the measurement factor becomes zt = At⊤βt + h(pt) + St + ηt with St the FOGM state, and the measurement Jacobian row is Ht = [gt⊤  0  At⊤  1], gt = ∂h/∂p. Because the batch solution uses the entire window, early navigation states benefit from calibration information that only becomes observable after later maneuvers—a property a causal filter lacks.

B. Fixed-lag sliding window

A single batch over a long line forces one static β, which underfits time-varying interference. We instead process the flight in overlapping windows of length Lw with overlap Lo, each a batch factor graph, in the manner of incremental smoothing [8]. A window commits only its leading stride Lw−Lo epochs and carries the full smoothed state and covariance forward as the prior for the next window; the trailing overlap serves as smoother look-ahead. The compensation thus adapts along the flight while navigation stays continuous across windows.

C. Physical sensor-error factors

Beyond TL we cast physically-motivated scalar-magnetometer error terms as graph variables: an optically-pumped-magnetometer heading error as a Fourier series in the sensor–field angle θ, a fluxgate hard-iron bias, a linear drift, and a dead-zone heteroscedastic measurement weight. A position-only grid or point-mass estimator cannot carry such sensor-error states; the graph formulation can.

IV.  Observability of Joint Compensation and Navigation

Whether position can be recovered while the compensation is estimated jointly is a property of the measurement geometry. Over a window let G have rows gt⊤ (the map-gradient/position sensitivity) and let Ψ have rows At⊤ (the TL regressor). Restricting (1) to the (δp, δβ) subspace gives the window model z = G δp + Ψ δβ + η.

Proposition 1. A position perturbation δp is indistinguishable from a compensation adjustment—hence unobservable from map matching over the window—if and only if G δp ∈ range(Ψ). Consequently the joint state (δp, δβ) is observable if and only if range(G) ∩ range(Ψ) = {0}.
Proof. Two joint states (δp, δβ) and (δp′, δβ′) produce identical noise-free predictions iff G(δp−δp′) = Ψ(δβ′−δβ), i.e. iff G(δp−δp′) ∈ range(Ψ). A nonzero position difference with this property exists iff range(G) ∩ range(Ψ) ≠ {0}, the negation of the stated condition; equivalently the stacked Jacobian [G Ψ] is column-rank-deficient. □
Corollary 1 (Exogeneity). If At is a function of aircraft attitude/fluxgate alone—exogenous to position—then range(Ψ) is generically transverse to the map-gradient subspace range(G) and the joint problem is observable at any basis dimension. If instead a compensation feature contains the map value h(p) (for example the scalar magnetometer itself), its column aligns with range(G), forcing the intersection nonzero and collapsing observability.

Proposition 1 is exact but non-constructive: it does not yield a reliable scalar screening statistic, and in our experiments correlation-based proxies for the intersection did not generalize across estimators. The actionable consequence is Corollary 1: drive the compensation from exogenous features, which every estimator in Section VI does.

V.  Reproduced Online EKF+TL+NN Baseline

To compare against [5] on equal footing we re-run, rather than cite, an online EKF with TL basis features and NN weights carried as EKF states and learned online. Reproducing the cold start required three fixes, each addressing a distinct failure: initializing the NN output bias to the onboard-estimated interference offset; restricting the NN inputs to the attitude-derived TL basis and excluding the scalar magnetometer (consistent with Corollary 1); and de-trusting the map through the cold-start transient. The resulting filter lands inside the paper’s published band and serves as our baseline.

VI.  Experiments and Results

All experiments use the public SGL 2020 dataset [3] and the Ottawa anomaly maps, and are produced by continuous-integration scripts on the code branch. The metric is horizontal distance-RMS (DRMS) after a 10-min warm-up. Cold start means TL coefficients initialized to zero with no calibration flight; the EKF and factor-graph comparisons use identical noise settings so that only the estimator structure differs.

A. Batch estimator on real data

On line 1003.02 (Flt1003, 63 min) with the compensated stinger magnetometer, the batch factor graph improves horizontal DRMS from the EKF’s 28.3 m to 14.0 m (Huber kernel), against 114.6 m free-inertial. On a 10-min segment the iterated-RTS and sparse-GN/QR solvers agree to within 1 m, confirming both reach the same MAP estimate.

B. Cold-start line vs. online EKF+TL+NN

Table I reports the primary evaluation line 1007.06 (87 min, full length), on the two uncompensated cabin magnetometers. A static-TL batch underfits and degrades over the long line. The fixed-lag window recovers it, and with a Huber kernel reaches 32.6 / 14.2 m. The reproduced online EKF+TL+NN reaches 40.0 / 17.5 m, inside the band published for that filter [5]. The window smoother thus matches or beats the NN-augmented filter without a neural network.

C. Breadth across lines, flights, and maps

Table II runs the same cold-start TL model through the causal online-TL EKF and the fixed-lag window smoother on five long survey lines spanning three flights and two maps. The window smoother is better on 8 of 9 cold-start cases. On the noisy Mag 4 the causal EKF diverges from the cold start to tens of kilometers on three lines, whereas the window smoother stays bounded, because its per-window relinearization lets later-observable calibration protect earlier navigation. The lone exception is a 14-min line on which every method is weak, reflecting a map-information floor for short lines.

D. Sensor-error factors (simulation)

On a simulated flight with injected heading error, hard-iron bias, drift, and dead-zone noise (INS reference 31.1 m), cumulatively adding the sensor-error factors reduces DRMS from 36.6 m (no sensor states, worse than INS because unmodeled error corrupts aiding) to 4.8 m. The drift and one bias component are recovered cleanly, while the heading harmonics are observability-limited because level flight sweeps the sensor–field angle by only about 12°—an observability property, not an estimator defect.

VII.  Discussion

The advantage is not smoothing per se, which equals RTS in the linear–Gaussian case, but the joint windowed estimation of a time-varying compensation with navigation. A causal filter must commit online with no future context; under a large uncompensated cold-start residual it attributes the mismatch to position and diverges, as Table II shows. The fixed-lag window defers commitment. Proposition 1 explains when the joint problem is well-posed and Corollary 1 gives the practical rule the results obey. Limitations include single-run DRMS on five lines (Monte-Carlo statistics and the full line set remain), the absence of a like-for-like grid/point-mass or particle-filter baseline, and simulation-only sensor-error validation. Short lines remain hard for every method.

VIII.  Conclusion

Casting online Tolles–Lawson aeromagnetic compensation as time-varying factor-graph variables in a fixed-lag sliding-window smoother yields a neural-network-free cold-start calibrator that, on real SGL 2020 data, matches or beats a reproduced online EKF+TL+NN on the primary line and generalizes across five lines, three flights, and two maps, remaining bounded where the causal EKF diverges. An exact observability condition and its exogeneity corollary explain the behavior and guide the choice of compensation features.
